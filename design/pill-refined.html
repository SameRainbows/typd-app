Typd — refinement pass 01

Same two circles. Sharper everything between them.

Nothing here is a new idea. The pill stays 36 tall, stays fully round, keeps a cancel on the left and a commit on the right, and keeps the monochrome ramp. What changed is the twenty or so places where the current build is approximately right instead of exactly right — the saturate that pulls the host application's colour into a monochrome design, the meter that fakes motion with a sine wave, the width morph that swaps its contents instead of moving them, the hairline that goes soft at 150%. Both pills below run off one state machine and one amplitude signal, so anything you can see is a real difference.

03 — What changed, and why it is better

Ordered by how much of the difference each one accounts for. Five things I interrogated and left alone are at the bottom; those are findings too, and two of them are the reason this is a refinement rather than a redesign.

01

saturate(1.6) removed from the backdrop filter cut

Saturation on a backdrop filter takes whatever hue is behind the pill and pushes it into the pill. Over a terminal the pill goes green, over a red logo it goes pink. A design whose entire premise is monochrome cannot have a filter that imports the host application's colour, and at 95% fill the effect it was buying was invisible anyway — you only ever saw the failure case. Switch the backdrop to Busy: the current pill picks up a wash, the refined one does not.

The blur went 18px → 12px at the same time. At a 97% fill the blur is insurance against high-frequency content bleeding through the last 3%, not a look, and a smaller radius is a cheaper compositing pass on a transparent always-on-top window.

02

The meter is a scrolling peak history, not eleven bars wiggling

The current row moves even when the level is constant, because each bar multiplies the amplitude by 0.68 + 0.32·sin(t·8.6 + phase[i]). That is decoration dressed as data — the row looks busy whether or not anything is happening, which is precisely the "AI slop" register the brief rules out. It also runs eleven bars through 56px of slot, which after device-pixel snapping is two pixels of ink with three of gap: a fence, and at that density it reads as noise rather than as a level.

Refined: nine slots across 58px, 3px of ink each, and every bar holds the peak level of one real 52ms window. New samples enter at the right and the row glides left continuously — sub-slot interpolation, so it scrolls rather than steps — with the slot leaving on the left dissolving instead of being clipped. Variation now comes from your actual voice, which produces a far more characterful silhouette than any sine wave ever will, and a moving row now means something is happening. The centre-weight — bars artificially shorter at the edges — had to go with it: in a history every bar must be comparable to every other, so the silhouette comes from the edge fade rather than from lying about amplitude.

One more thing fell out of rewriting it: the shipped meter computes maxHalf = h / 2, so a bar at full level is exactly as tall as the canvas and its rounded caps are clipped flat against the top and bottom edges. The loudest syllable is the one moment the meter stops being round. design/overlay-prototype.html had this right — h / 2 - barW / 2 — and it was lost on the way into overlay.js. Restored.

03

Controls leave instead of vanishing; the morph became one object

The reason the current morph reads as objects swapping is not the width transition — that part is fine. It is that the children use display: none. At the instant of a state change the tick is gone in one frame and the text is present in one frame, while the box takes 240ms to travel. Two different clocks, so the eye separates them into two things.

Refined: an exiting control animates its own flex-basis, width, margin, opacity and scale on the same 260ms spring as the pill, so you watch it get folded away and the pill close over it. Meter and text are stacked in one slot and cross-fade in place (100ms out, 180ms in at an 80ms delay) rather than replacing each other in the layout. Nothing hard-cuts anywhere.

04

Thinking is 102px, not 98 — so the meter never changes width

132 − 102 = 30 = one 24px control plus its 6px margin, exactly. The old 130 → 98 was 32, which meant the meter also shrank by 2px on the way in and grew by 2px on the way out. Two pixels is not visible as a size change; it is visible as the row of bars resampling, which is why the thinking transition felt slightly unstable. Now the only thing that moves is the right end of the pill, and the sentence the animation says is "the tick left and the door closed behind it".

Error width went 216 → 246, the same as result and streaming. 216 and 252 are indistinguishable to the eye but cost a real morph whenever a session ends badly. One fewer arbitrary number.

05

The hairline holds at 150% and 200% Windows scaling

36px is the correct height at every scaling factor, because DPI scaling preserves physical size — the pill is the same size in the hand at 100% and 150%. What breaks at 150% is not the size, it is the border: 1 CSS px × 1.5 = 1.5 device px, which lands on a half pixel and is composited as a soft two-pixel smear. That softness is most of the reason the current pill reads as slightly out of focus next to Wispr's on a scaled display.

Refined declares --hair and steps it down as resolution goes up — 1px, then 0.667px at 1.5dppx, then 0.5px at 2dppx — so the border is exactly one device pixel everywhere. Set the scale control to 150% and look at the two edges. The same variable drives the ::before edge ring, so it does not drift out of register with the border either.

06

The self-drawing tick is gone cut

A 360ms stroke-dash animation on the most repeated interaction in the product. You see it every single time you press the hotkey, forty times a day, and a flourish at that frequency stops being charming and becomes a stutter between you and the button. The pill's own entrance already carries the arrival; the tick should simply be there when it lands.

Worth noting for the record: the path is 15.6 units long and the stroke-dasharray was 22, so 29% of the animation's duration was spent moving a dash offset that had already cleared the end of the line. Nobody noticed, which is the argument.

07

Icons authored in device pixels, and made heavier

A 12px <svg> on a 24-unit viewBox halves every number in the path: stroke-width: 2.4 ships as a 1.2px line, and the cross itself spans 5px inside a 24px disc. At 1:1 that is a grey smudge, and it is why the cancel control reads as absent rather than as quiet.

Refined renders the SVG at 24px on a 24-unit viewBox, so one unit is one pixel and the numbers in the markup are the numbers on screen. The cross is an 8×8 box with a 1.7px stroke; the tick is 1.9px, half a step heavier because it is the primary action and weight is how a monochrome design says primary. Compare them in the 4× strips.

08

direction: rtl replaced with a scroll pin and an edge fade

Holding the newest words in view by flipping the writing direction is clever and it works right up until the transcript ends in punctuation. Trailing full stops, commas and quotes are bidi-neutral, so RTL relocates them to the far left of the pill — the streaming text renders as .model the of end the at-style artefacts on perfectly ordinary sentences. Play a session and watch the current pill at the moment the stream finishes.

Refined keeps LTR, pins the tail with justify-content: flex-end plus a scroll nudge, and fades the overflowing edge with a mask instead of an ellipsis. The fade is also simply better: an ellipsis is a glyph that says "truncated", a fade is the text going out of the window.

09

The shadow stack lost half its reach; dark lost most of it

The ambient layer was 0 16px 40px under a 36px-tall object — a shadow larger than the thing casting it, which over a busy background is not depth, it is a smudge. Refined runs 1/1 · 4/10 · 12/26, still three layers doing contact, lift and ambient, but sized to the object.

In dark mode the old stack ran black at .45–.55 alpha under a near-black pill on a near-black background: expensive, and invisible. What separates a dark pill from a dark backdrop is the lit top edge and the border, not the shadow, so dark now spends its contrast budget there — border to .145, top edge to .16 — and its shadows are cut back.

10

The sheen became a real edge, in both themes

--pill-sheen: rgba(255,255,255,0.85) painted on a rgba(252,252,253,0.95) pill. White on white. In light mode the ::before was a mask-composite operation producing no pixels.

Refined runs one vertical gradient through a full-perimeter 1px ring: light at the top, transparent through the middle, shaded at the bottom. Light mode now gets a defined lower lip, dark mode keeps the lit top edge it always needed, and it is one declaration instead of a per-theme special case.

11

Silence is a dotted rule; thinking got quieter

The prototype's note that a flat meter "read as broken" is correct and the idle floor stays. What goes is the floor's own sine wobble: silence should be still, so that the first syllable is the only thing that has ever moved. At rest the nine bars settle to 3px dots at mid-height — a dotted rule, which reads as a live input at rest rather than as a dead one.

The thinking sweep peaked at 0.87 of full scale, meaning the pill was more animated while waiting than while you were talking. That inverts the hierarchy. Capped at 0.55 and slowed from ~1.07s to ~1.25s: present, unmistakably working, subordinate to your voice.

12

Segoe UI Variable Text, not Display, at 12.5px

Segoe UI Variable ships four optical sizes and Display is cut for 24pt and up: tight sidebearings, closed apertures, fine joins. Setting it at 12px is using the wrong end of the family, and it is why the result text looks a little brittle at actual size. Text is the cut for exactly this range.

Size 12 → 12.5px and the result weight 560 → 600, on the reasoning that a fraction more size buys more legibility at a glance than a fraction more weight does, and that this string is read peripherally in under a second. Also removed: -webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased, which is a macOS-only property and has never done anything on Windows.

13

The entrance is 200ms and travels less far

260ms, 8px of rise and scale(.92) is a considered entrance for a dialog. This is chrome that has to be present before you have finished pressing the hotkey. 200ms, 6px, scale(.96) — same spring, same character, arrives ahead of your reaction time instead of alongside it. The smaller scale also matters mechanically: scaling a 1px border by .92 makes it 0.92px for the whole entrance, so a shorter throw is a sharper edge.

14

The error state keeps its cancel control

Currently error hides both controls and sits there for 2.2 seconds with no way to dismiss it — the one state where a user actively wants the thing gone is the one state that gives them nothing to press. The hit-testing in overlay.js already skips zero-width controls, so keeping the ✕ visible costs one selector and no logic.

15

Two loose ends worth flagging, not fixed here

overlay.js writes data-mode="dictate|revise" on every session and the stylesheet never reads it, with a comment saying the pill "must not look identical" in revision. That is an unfinished thought, and the honest monochrome answer is a small label above the pill (the prototype had one) rather than anything applied to the pill itself. Deciding that is a design question, not a refinement, so it is out of scope here.

The :focus-visible rule is unreachable: both controls carry tabindex="-1" and the window never takes focus. Harmless, but it is a rule that will be maintained forever by someone who assumes it works.

—

Interrogated and left alone already right

The 36 / 24 / 6 geometry is exact, and I would not touch it. A 24px disc inset 6px in a 36px pill puts its centre at (18, 18) — which is also the centre of the pill's end-cap arc. The two circles are concentric, and the gap between the disc and the pill's edge is a uniform 6px ring the whole way around, not 6px at the sides and something else at the corner. That is deliberate, correct and rare; the only change is that the pill is now 132 wide, which puts the thinking state on a whole number and gives the meter 58px instead of 56.

Full radius (h/2 = 18) is right. Anything less makes it a widget with rounded corners. Fully round is what makes it read as OS chrome, and it is the one thing every good pill in this category agrees on.

Animating width is right here, despite the standing advice never to animate layout properties. A transform-based fake would scale the border, the radius and the children along with it; on a 132px box with two children the layout cost is nil, and correctness beats the rule of thumb.

The canvas device-pixel snapping in overlay.js is good work. Rounding geometry to whole device pixels, filling rounded rects rather than stroking lines, and refusing any shadow near the meter is exactly why the bars are crisp. The refined meter inherits all three unchanged; only what the bars mean changed.

The narrow-then-wide beat is a good idea. 132 → 102 → 246 is an inhale before speaking, and it makes the arrival of the transcript feel earned. Kept exactly, with only the arithmetic corrected.

Near-opaque fill over real translucency is the right trade. The pill has to be legible over a black terminal and a white document with the same numbers. That constraint has one answer and it is a solid object; the fill went up, not down.

One thing I considered and rejected. The brief asks whether the pill itself could carry the level instead of a meter — an edge or a fill that responds to your voice. It could, and it would be the single most distinctive thing on this page. It is also a 132px object with a one-device-pixel border pulsing at syllable rate three inches from where you are reading, and in every version I sketched it read as a glow. The meter stays a bounded region, because the part of the pill that is an object should never be the part that moves.
