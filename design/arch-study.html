Typd — bottom arch study 01

The edge swells, then recedes. It morphs in height, not in width.

A pill floats above the taskbar, which is what an application does. An arch grows out of the screen edge, which is what infrastructure does. The interesting question is not whether that reads better — it does — but what it costs, and the answer turns out to be a specific number of occluded pixels and one hard collision with the Windows taskbar. Everything below is at 1:1.

meeting-notes.md — Notepad

Thursday review — what actually blocks us

The recogniser is not the bottleneck and has not been for two weeks. Parakeet comes back in about 350 ms on a warm process, which is well inside the window where nobody notices, and the word error we measured is 7.2%. What people are actually reacting to is the second half of the pipeline.

Cleanup is where the time goes. First token lands around 550 ms, and because the overlay only starts revealing text at first token, every millisecond of that is dead air with a sweep animation in it. Nobody has complained about the sweep. Several people have complained that it runs for too long.

The obvious move is to stream the recogniser's own output into the overlay immediately and let the cleaned version replace it in place, which trades one kind of latency for one kind of flicker.

So the question for Thursday is whether we are willing to pay that flicker

The two variants

A · Sill — 560 wide, 40–48 tall

A long, shallow swell with a flat crown, shoulders melting tangentially into the edge over 168 px each. Reads as the bottom of the display deforming rather than as an object arriving.

The trade: it is correct for every state and it looks like almost nothing — at 48 px of rise across 560 px it is a swell, not an arch, and it occludes a 560 px band of whatever you were reading.

B · Dome — 240 wide, 72–84 tall

Short span, real curvature, an actual arch. It is the better-looking object by a wide margin, it is the one that photographs well, and its rise change between states is genuinely legible in peripheral vision.

The trade: it cannot hold a line of text. The chord at text height measures 153 px, so Pasted into Notepad lands in a 125 px slot and ellipsises; in streaming, where the cancel disc is still on screen, it collapses to 68 px. Step to Streaming and watch it fail.

Why the shoulders are cubics, not arcs

A circular arc meets the baseline at an angle, which draws two visible corners at the screen edge, and two corners is a shape sitting on the edge. The shoulders here are cubic Béziers with control points at .68 and .32, so the curve arrives at the baseline with zero slope and the last 25 px of each foot are within a pixel and a half of flat. There is no corner to see.

The hairline is masked to fade out over the lower half of each shoulder for the same reason: a stroke that terminates on the baseline puts the corner back.

Width or height — the actual answer

Height. The span must never change. The two feet are the arch's entire contract with the screen edge; they are what says this is where the display swells. Slide them and the arch stops being a deformation of the edge and becomes a widget that resizes itself, which is precisely the thing the pill already does and precisely the thing the concept was supposed to escape. A fixed span with a changing rise reads as breath. A changing span reads as a window being dragged.

There is a second reason, and it is the better one. With a fixed span, the controls can be pinned to fixed offsets from centre and the rise can change without moving a single thing the user is aiming at. Today the pill's 130 → 98 → 252 morph translates the cancel disc 16 px right and then 77 px left, twice per session, while the cursor may already be in flight toward it. Switch to Pill above and step Listening → Thinking → Streaming, watching only the cross. Then do the same on Sill. The cross does not move. For a control surface — and the README insists this is a control surface, not a status light — that is the strongest argument the arch has, and it is not an aesthetic one.

The cost of that decision is that the morph nearly disappears. The Sill's whole state range is 40 → 32 → 48 px of rise: eight pixels of extra headroom to say the model finished thinking. The pill's 130 → 252 is unmissable in peripheral vision; the Sill's is not. State stops being carried by the container and has to be carried entirely by content — the meter's sweep, the text arriving. That is a real loss and it is not recoverable without breaking the fixed span.

Does the arch beat the pill

Enter / exitArch, clearly The pill fakes its arrival with translateY(8px) scale(.92) — a transform standing in for a physical event. The arch's arrival is the physical event: rise tweens 0 → 40 on the same 260 ms spring and the edge genuinely swells. No transform, no scale, nothing to disbelieve.
ListeningArch The meter is already centre-weighted — 0.5 + 0.5·sin(πi/n) in overlay.js makes the middle bars taller. Put a centre-weighted row inside a centre-tall arch and the two silhouettes agree; inside a rectangle they argue. Turn Breathe on and the crown also carries a slow phrase-rate swell, well below syllable rate so it never jitters at the edge of vision.
ThinkingArch, mildly Rise drops 8 px and holds. Reads as held breath. The pill's 130 → 98 snap reads as the window shrinking, which is an odd thing for a machine to do while it is working. Both are quiet; the arch is quieter.
StreamingPill, or a wash Text wants one wide constant-height line. The Sill lands on 210 px — exactly the pill's slot, by coincidence of the disc constraint — so it is a wash, bought at twice the occluded area. The Dome lands on 68 px and is unusable.
ResultArch (Sill only) Both discs are hidden, so the whole chord is available: 364 px against the pill's 210. Pasted into Notepad never ellipsises, and longer app names stop being a problem. The Dome gets 125 px and is already failing.
ErrorArch (Sill only) Same geometry as Result, and error strings are the longest strings the overlay ever shows — the pill has to widen to 216 px and still truncates. The arch itself stays monochrome; only the text takes --danger. Colouring the arch would be the wrong instinct.

What it costs

Occlusion, not dead zone

The brief's worry — a big arch is a big dead zone — is false as the code stands. overControl(x, y) in overlay.js walks exactly two bounding rects plus a 5 px pad; the silhouette is never consulted. Turn on Hit regions: 2,312 px² with both controls up, 1,156 px² once confirm is hidden — identical in all three variants, arch or pill.

What the arch actually costs is visibility. The Sill occludes 2.0× the pill's area in streaming and 3.4× in listening, in a band across the bottom of whatever you were reading — which, in an editor, is often the line you are dictating into.

The taskbar is the real problem

positionOverlay parks against display.workArea, and workArea stops at the top of the taskbar. So "the bottom edge of the screen" is, by default, a seam with its own border and its own fill. Toggle Anchor line: the arch's feet melt into a line that is already there, and the illusion is half dead on arrival.

Fixing it means display.bounds instead — toggle Display edge. Now it genuinely grows out of the screen, and it also grows over Start and the pinned icons, and it needs ~48 px more rise to clear them. Auto-hide taskbars and side-docked taskbars break it outright. Pick one; there is no third option.

Two smaller bills

Compositing. A 560 px backdrop-filter: blur(18px) on an always-on-top window with backgroundThrottling: false is roughly 4× the blurred surface of the pill, running whenever the overlay is up. Measure it before committing; it is the kind of thing that only shows up on a laptop on battery.

Ask Anything. That mode wants a card above the control, and a 400 px card sitting on a 560 px arch is bottom-heavy in a way the 130 px pill never was. The arch is a bigger commitment than the state it currently has to serve.

Verdict

Ship the Sill, or ship nothing. Do not ship the Dome.

The Dome is the better object and the worse product. It is the one that looks like an arch, it is the one worth a screenshot, and it cannot hold a line of text — 68 px in streaming is not a near miss, it is a different feature. That failure is structural, not a tuning problem: an arch's interior is widest at the baseline and narrowest at the crown, so text wants the baseline — which is the one place it can never sit, because the baseline is the screen edge. Every arch is a container that is widest exactly where it is least usable. A rounded rectangle has no such gradient, which is the unglamorous reason the pill keeps winning the text states.

Now the uncomfortable part. Follow every pressure honestly — text needs width, so widen; occlusion needs restraint, so flatten; controls need to stop moving, so fix the span — and you arrive at 560 × 48. That is not an arch. It is a swell, and it is most of the way back to a very wide rounded rectangle. The design converges on the pill from the other side. What survives the convergence is exactly one property: the thing is attached. It has no floating gap under it, no drop shadow pretending it hovers, no bottom edge at all. That single property is the entire thesis of the README, and the Sill is the cheapest honest way to buy it.

So the recommendation is narrow. Take the edge-anchoring, drop the arch. Keep the pill's rectangular interior for text, remove the 6 px LIFT, and let the box's bottom corners run out into tangential shoulders that dissolve into the edge — which is what the Sill already is at low rise. You get the infrastructure read, the fixed controls, and the wider Result and Error slots, and you pay 2× occlusion and one irreversible decision about the taskbar. If the taskbar decision cannot be made — if auto-hide has to keep working — the concept is not shippable and the pill stays, because an arch growing out of a seam is worse than a pill that never claimed to be attached in the first place.

One thing this study does not settle: none of it has been seen against a maximised video, a game, or a light-on-dark IDE at 125% scaling, which is where a 560 px band across the bottom edge is most likely to become intolerable. That is a build-it-and-live-with-it question, not a prototype question.
