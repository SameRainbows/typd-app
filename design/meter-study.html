Typd · design study · meter

Scrolling history or bouncing bars?

Four meters reading the same input. A is the meter Typd ships, ported line for line. B keeps A's signal and changes only the layout, to the fixed bouncing bars most rivals use. C is the candidate: fixed bars on a dB scale measured from the room's noise floor, a fast rise and a slow fall, and an optional faint peak-hold. D is the smallest honest meter there is: one line. Speak, or run the demo signal, then try Silence and the two Dead mic inputs.

Verdict, 2026-10-09

For a pill that is never looked at, the fixed meter is better, not just different. The scrolling row moves whether or not anyone speaks: in silence it moves a third as much ink as it does during speech, and more than the fixed layout moves while you talk. Its first visible response comes one push later (67 ms against 17 ms), and it keeps gliding over a dead microphone. Travelling motion is also the most distracting kind in peripheral-vision studies. Keep C, without the peak-hold. Scrolling still suits screens people actually watch, like Voice Memos, Recorder, or a composer field.

What each meter is

A · Typd today

  • RMS of the last 64 ms, divided by a peak that decays ×0.995 per frame with a 0.015 floor (linear auto-gain), then smoothed ×0.4 per frame.
  • The peak of each 52 ms window is pushed in on the right; 9 visible slots glide left continuously.
  • Quirks kept: lastPush = now makes a push land on the first frame ≥ 52 ms, which is every 4th frame (66.7 ms) at 60 Hz. Three frames glide a third of a slot each, the fourth barely moves: a 15 Hz hitch. The glide runs in silence, so the dotted rule drifts at about one slot per push. The canvas ignores reduced motion.

B · Rival layout, Typd's signal

  • The same level as A, frame for frame, drawn as 9 fixed bars × a centre-weighted profile (0.46 at the edges, 1.0 in the middle).
  • The only variable that changes from A is layout, so A vs B isolates "scroll vs bounce".
  • Working: a swell travels across fixed bars, capped at 0.55, as in pill-refined.html.

C · Candidate

  • Level is the RMS of the last 32 ms in dBFS. Zero height is the room's noise floor (the minimum over the last 2 s, ignoring digital zeros) + 6 dB, and never below −70 dBFS. Full height is 36 dB above that.
  • One-pole ballistics: 15 ms attack, 160 ms release; outer bars up to 2.5× slower to rise, so an onset blooms from the centre.
  • Peak-hold (optional): a faint copy of each bar holds 600 ms, then falls at 1.6 full-scale/s.
  • Exact zeros for 600 ms dim the dots to 35%: a dead mic looks different from a quiet room, even mid-session.
  • Reduced motion: no geometry changes; the dots brighten while you speak (400 ms hold) and dim in pauses.

D · One line

  • C's level and ballistics, drawn as one centred 3 px line that grows from a 6 px dash.
  • The least ink and motion per bit of information; also the least like a "voice" glyph.
  • Reduced motion: opacity only, as C.

Why motion in silence costs something

  • Travelling motion was rated by far the most distracting peripheral cue; slow in-place motion the least (Bartram, Ware & Calvert 2003).
  • A continuously scrolling ticker cut editing performance by 43%, against 26% for one that moved in steps (Maglio & Campbell 2000).
  • A change captures attention when it breaks a calm (von Mühlenen et al. 2005). Still dots make the first syllable that change; a moving row masks it.
  • Jerky motion (8–17 Hz refresh) captures attention and smooth motion does not (Sunny & von Mühlenen 2011). A's push lands on every 4th frame at 60 Hz, a 15 Hz hitch.
  • Unlooked-at banners still raised workload (Burke et al. 2005). Not noticing a cost is not the same as it being free.

Why the meter must follow input, and only input

  • An OpenWhispr user talked for about seven minutes into a dead mic because the bars animated regardless of input (#846). FreeFlow has the same report (#313).
  • An OpenAI forum user says ChatGPT's scrolling visualiser keeps moving after you stop, which reads as a live mic.
  • Handy moved its window to −68…−30 dB; −70 was rejected because a noisy room made idle bars twitch (PR #1813). Hence C's gate sits above the measured floor.
  • Willow removed its quiet-mic warnings as "more noise than help" (Mac v2.3.10). Let the meter say "too quiet" instead.
  • Visual lag behind your own voice is detectable from about 40–45 ms and unacceptable past about 90 ms (ITU-R BT.1359; Levitin et al.).
  • A scrolling volume plot ranked last of the live speech-feedback displays tested (Rhema, Tanveer et al. 2015).
